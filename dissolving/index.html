What decides whether an ionic compound dissolves?

Water is a polar molecule. Its oxygen end is attracted to positive ions and its hydrogen end to negative ions. These ion–dipole attractions tug on the ions at the surface of the crystal.

An ion leaves when the pull from several water molecules overcomes the ionic bonds holding it in the lattice. Once free it is surrounded by a shell of oriented water molecules. It is now hydrated, written (aq).

Whether a compound is soluble comes down to a balance. Energy is needed to break up the lattice and energy is released when the ions are hydrated. Dissolving also usually increases entropy as the ordered lattice becomes freely moving ions. When the ionic bonds are very strong compared with the hydration, as in AgCl, very few ions escape.

In a saturated solution with solid still present, ions leave and rejoin the crystal at the same rate. This is a dynamic equilibrium, described by Ksp.
