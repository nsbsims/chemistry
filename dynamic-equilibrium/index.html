Questions and notes

Check your understanding questions
Static vs dynamic equilibrium

Static equilibrium: nothing is changing and nothing is happening. The forward and reverse rates are both zero. A book resting on a table is in static equilibrium. So is the water model if you simply stop transferring.

Dynamic equilibrium: nothing appears to change (colour, pressure and concentrations are constant) because the forward and reverse reactions are happening at equal, non-zero rates. The tagged atom shows this: it keeps switching between N₂O₄ and NO₂ long after the graph has gone flat.

At equilibrium the concentrations are constant, not necessarily equal.

A limit of this model: the box holds only 100 nitrogen atoms, so the counts wobble around the equilibrium values. A real flask holds around 10²³ molecules, which is why real concentration–time graphs are smooth.

Open vs closed systems

A closed system exchanges energy with its surroundings but not matter. An open system exchanges both.

Equilibrium needs a closed system. Take the lid off and gas escapes, so the reverse reaction keeps losing the particles it needs and the system never settles.
