Module 5 · Calculating the Equilibrium Constant

Virtual Colorimeter Prac

Determine Keq for the iron(III) thiocyanate equilibrium. Build a calibration line from standards, measure five equilibrium mixtures, then process your own data. Every reading has realistic random error.

Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq)pale yellow + colourless ⇌ blood red

  1. Zero the colorimeter with the blank.
  2. Measure standards S1 to S5. In these, Fe³⁺ is in huge excess, so assume all the SCN⁻ becomes FeSCN²⁺.
  3. Measure equilibrium mixtures M1 to M5.
  4. Use the calibration line to find [FeSCN²⁺] in each mixture, then use an ICE table to find K.

About this model and its limits

The absorbance of each solution is calculated from the true equilibrium concentration of FeSCN²⁺ using the Beer–Lambert law, with a small random error added to every reading. Repeat a measurement and you will get a slightly different value.

A built-in systematic error: the method assumes that every SCN⁻ ion in a standard becomes FeSCN²⁺. Even with at least a 500-fold excess of Fe³⁺, a few percent stays unreacted, so the true concentrations of the standards are slightly lower than assumed. This makes your calibration line slightly too shallow and your value of K slightly too high. The same thing happens in the real experiment.

Your calculations are checked against your own measurements, so there is no single right number.
